This notice sets out how the Find a Tender service (FTS) will use your personal data and your rights in relation to that data. It is made under Articles 13 and 14 of the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR).

## Your data

### Purpose
   
The Find a Tender service (FTS) is provided by the Cabinet Office. For more information, please visit the Cabinet Office’s entry in the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) Registration of Fee Payers. Contracting authorities act as joint data controllers with the Cabinet Office for this service. 

The purpose of FTS is to publish procurement notices and related information as required by the Procurement Act 2023 (the Act) and Procurement Regulations 2024 (the Procurement Regulations) and other relevant procurement regimes, for example the Public Contracts Regulations 2015. 

It is also to facilitate participation in public procurement under the Act 2023 where contracting authorities require suppliers to register with FTS and complete their 'supplier information' through the Supplier Information and Registration System (SIRSI). This supplier information can then be shared along with a declaration by the supplier to the relevant contracting authority, to be processed and reviewed by the contracting authority at the time of tendering. 

FTS also hosts other services to support or enhance public procurement processes for contracting authorities. These include tools that use artificial intelligence to help contracting authorities generate statements of work, assure statements of requirement, compile answers to Clarification Questions or assure Key Performance Indicators of public contracts. The Cabinet Office is an independent data controller for personal data processed on these tools.

# **Collection, use and disclosure of additional personal information** 

# **Personal data of connected people** 

We collect personal data from those “connected people” that you are obliged to provide information on, as set out in [regulation 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/692/regulation/11) as part of your supplier information. This can then be shared along with a declaration by you to the relevant contracting authority, to be processed and reviewed by the contracting authority at the time of tendering. This information will not be shared by the Cabinet Office with other contracting authorities. 

It is your responsibility to inform your connected persons that this information has been submitted. The data we collect is: 

* name 

* address 

* date of birth 

* nationality 

* country of residence 

* associations with legal entities 

# **Publication of connected person information** 

When publishing a contract award notice, contracting authorities have a duty to publish information about your associated person(s) (as defined in [section 26(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/26/enacted)) and connected person(s) (as set out in [regulation 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/692/regulation/11)). For associated persons this may include personal data in the form of their name and contact postal address if the name and address are of an individual. Depending on the type of connected person, this may include their name, date of birth (month and year only), nationality and service address (which could be the same as their personal address). 

Contracting authorities are obliged to check with you that the details you have shared on connected persons are publishable, or if they are exempted from publication in accordance with [Regulation 27(4-6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/692/regulation/27) which provides for circumstances where disclosure of information about a connected person could place that person or anyone living with them at risk<sup>3</sup> . 

Where a connected person is a person with significant control (PSC) under the Companies Act 2006, and information relating to them is omitted from the PSC register established

under that Act in accordance with [regulation 33(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/339/regulation/33) of the 2016 Regulations, a contracting authority is not required to publish their information. 

Where a connected person is not a PSC, a contracting authority is nevertheless not required to publish their information where that individual has confirmed in writing to the contracting authority that they are reasonably of the view that: 

a. the activities of the company to whom they are connected; or 

b. one or more characteristics or personal attributes of the applicant when associated with that company; 

will put the applicant or a person living with the applicant at serious risk of being subjected to violence or intimidation. Where information about connected persons is protected from disclosure on the PSC register under the 2016 Regulations, contracting authorities are required to indicate that there is a PSC (by stating this in the relevant notice) but not provide any details. 

To the extent that this information has been published, your connected person information may be used within tools such as search facilities, dashboards, analytics to support business processes and will be provided in publicly available data outputs. 

# **Criminal convictions personal data** 

We and the contracting authority to whom you submit your data may process personal data you have provided relating to criminal convictions and offences, or suspicion of the commission of an offence, for yourself and those 'connected people' who you are obliged to provide information on, to facilitate compliance with the Act 2023. This information may then be used to determine whether any exclusion grounds for the purposes of public procurement apply. 

Where relevant, contracting authorities are required to publish the following information in line with the requirements of the Act 2023 and Procurement Regulations. This includes publication of transparency notices pursuant to [regulation 26](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/692/regulation/26) which requires the authority to confirm whether the contract is being awarded directly to a supplier that is an excluded supplier in accordance with section 41(2) to (5) of the Act 2023, the offence or other event mentioned in Schedule 6 to the Act 2023 by virtue of which the supplier is an excluded supplier, which ground in section 41(5) of the Act 2023 applies and an explanation of why the contracting authority considers that it applies. 

Information may also be published  in the event that you as a supplier are placed on the debarment list under the process set out in [section 62](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/62/enacted) of the Act). Any information published may be used within tools such as search facilities, dashboards, analytics to support business processes and will be provided in publicly available data outputs. 

The criminal convictions data we may collect on individuals connected to the supplier organisation relate to the grounds set out in Schedules [6](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/schedule/6/enacted) and [7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/schedule/7/enacted) of the Procurement Act 2023. Personal data relating to connected people and criminal convictions will not be disclosed or shared for any other purpose than those specified above. 

# **Google Analytics** 

We collect some analytical data on use of the digital service. We use Google Analytics to collect information about how you use FTS. Google Analytics collects your IP Address and assigns to your device a cookie containing a randomly generated unique identifier called ‘Client ID’. This is your personal data. When using the same device to access FTS, we will use Google Analytics to track the pages visited and any links you select. 

We will only track your activity in this way if you have given consent. You can control whether Google Analytics tracks you by adjusting your cookies. 

Our lawful basis for processing your analytics data is consent ([Article 6 (1)(a) UK GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/)). 

# **Recipients of your data** 

For all users, the Cabinet Office has access to your data in line with this privacy notice. In respect of your basic personal information as set out above, we may share this with Contracting Authorities and their e-procurement service providers in order to facilitate compliance with the above legislation, and for ensuring that records held are accurate. 

In addition, if you are a supplier, any contracting authority to whom you have shared your data in order to participate in public procurement will have access to your data. This includes the data of any connected people and any criminal convictions you as the supplier have input into the service. This data may be shared with the contracting authority’s e-procurement and related service providers and to the extent set out above, may be published in notices and other procurement information. Where this is the case, this data may be used within tools such as search facilities, dashboards, analytics to support business processes and will be provided in publicly available data outputs. 

We will also share your data when necessary with our suppliers. These comprise our cloud hosting provider, GOV.UK One Login (to verify your identity), GovNotify (for the sending of emails to you), our web analytics provider (if you have consented to cookies), our reporting dashboard supplier and our technical support teams.

# **Data retention** 

We will review your account if you have not used it for 7 years, your account will be closed and your account information will be deleted. 

We are required to retain data published on FTS in order to meet the obligations of the Procurement Act 2023, which require us to establish and maintain this platform. It is important to note that any information published on the central digital platform is considered to be ‘in the public domain’ and, while information on the platform can be removed, the Cabinet Office cannot control or be held responsible for any data that has already been accessed, copied, distributed, reused or exploited under the Open Government License prior to that information’s removal. 

# **Lawful basis for the processing** 

The legal basis for processing this personal data is [UK GDPR Article 6(1)(c)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/), namely that processing is necessary to comply with a legal obligation placed on us as the data controller. In this case that is the obligation of a Minister of the Crown (the Cabinet Office) to provide an online platform (Find a Tender service) for the publication of information required by the Act 2023 and for the collection and facilitation of sharing supplier information as required by the Procurement Regulations. 

The lawful basis for processing personal data for the AI tools provided to select contracting authorities is that it is necessary for a public function (aka the ‘public task’ basis). The suite of AI tools comprises Statement of Work assure, Statement of work generate, Statement of requirement assure, Statement of requirement generate, Clarification Questions and Key Performance Indicators. 

# **Your rights** 

You have the right: 

* to request information about how your personal data are processed, and to request a copy of that personal data 

* to request that any inaccuracies in your personal data are rectified without delay 

* to request that any incomplete personal data are completed, including by means of a supplementary statement 

* to request that your personal data are erased if there is no longer a justification for them to be processed 

* in certain circumstances (for example, where accuracy is contested) to request that the processing of your personal data is restricted 

* In relation to contacting authority users accessing the suite of AI tools, you have a right to object to the processing of your personal data. 

# **International transfers** 

All personal and sensitive data collected by FTS is shared with Amazon Web Services (AWS) and is stored in the UK. Data collected by Google Analytics may be transferred outside the European Economic Area (EEA) for processing. 

Where data processing happens in the US, suppliers need to meet the certification standards set out in the UK-US data bridge agreement. This facilitates flows of personal data to certified US companies that have opted into the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
